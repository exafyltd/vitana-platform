# Trust, Vitana Index and Reputation V1

**Status:** Conceptual specification; scoring methodology not approved. **Version:** 0.1.

## 1. Purpose
Represent quality, reliability and trust for people, providers and destinations in the Longevity Economy. Trust must be evidence-based, explainable, resistant to manipulation and fair to new participants. It must not be a popularity or paid-promotion score.

## 2. Three domains
- **Human Vitana Index:** health/wellbeing; an existing application concept using 0–999 and an existing algorithm. This document does not change it.
- **Provider Vitana Index:** professional/provider quality and trust; proposed, not treated as implemented.
- **Location Vitana Index:** destination quality and experience; proposed, not treated as implemented.

Direction: one shared index service/engine, specialized domain methodologies and a common 0–999 presentation scale. Shared scale does not mean identical evidence or algorithms.

## 3. Provider/location distinction
Provider index is portable across locations. A provider serving three destinations has one provider-level identity/index. Each location has its own Location Index and may include context-specific service evidence. Do not copy a provider score mechanically into a location score.

## 4. Candidate dimensions
Provider: identity/credentials, qualifications, expertise, service quality/consistency, reliability, customer experience, safety/compliance, verified outcomes where appropriate, contribution to Longevity Economy.

Location: verification/transparency, facilities, service/product quality, customer experience, reliability, safety/compliance, provider quality/service mix and ecosystem contribution.

Dimensions, evidence, eligibility and weights are open. Sub-indices may explain a resort's nutrition, fitness, sleep, medical-service or customer-experience quality, provided definitions and evidence are meaningful.

## 5. Evidence hierarchy
Potential evidence: verified identity/credentials, authoritative licences, verified service/transaction records, safety audits, structured verified feedback, longitudinal reliability and appropriately consented outcomes. Unverified marketing and anonymous popularity do not equal verified evidence. Paid placement never improves a score.

## 6. Fairness and sufficiency
Distinguish not assessed, insufficient evidence, provisional/limited confidence, assessed, suspended and under review. New participants must not receive a low score merely for having no history. Show confidence/evidence sufficiency separately from score.

## 7. Methodology requirements
Define domain dimensions, eligible evidence, verification, weighting, aggregation, recency/decay, minimum sample, confidence, missing-data behavior, outlier/manipulation detection, category normalization, appeals and methodology versioning. Previously discussed bands are proposals, not approved thresholds.

## 8. Explainability and appeals
Explain what is assessed, which dimensions contribute, verified evidence, missing information, freshness and improvement paths. Sensitive evidence is access-controlled. Providers/locations can correct facts, submit evidence and challenge score/evidence classification. Appeals are independent of promotional sales.

## 9. Integrity controls
Address fake reviews, coordinated manipulation, duplicate identities, credential fraud, undisclosed paid influence, suppression/retaliation, collusion and fabricated outcomes. Use anomaly detection and human review for consequential sanctions; do not make unsupported automated allegations.

## 10. Governance and safety
Human health, professional quality and location experience have different ethical/regulatory implications. Avoid diagnosis or unsupported clinical claims. Establish audit, bias monitoring and jurisdictional review.

## 11. Acceptance criteria
Domains remain distinct; provider score is portable; location score is independent; insufficient evidence is not low score; provenance/freshness are visible; methodology is explainable/versioned; paid promotion excluded; appeals/anti-manipulation defined; new entrants treated fairly.

## 12. Open decisions
Dimension taxonomy; evidence partners; weighting/aggregation; confidence/minimum evidence; decay/recalculation; public display; appeal governance; outcome/health-data relationship; sub-index aggregation; jurisdictional review.

**Guardrail:** do not invent or deploy weights from this conceptual specification. Map existing Human Index implementation and separately define Provider and Location methodologies.
