# Destination UX and Experience Framework V1

**Status:** Experience design baseline; patterns to be validated. **Version:** 0.1.

## 1. Purpose and principle
Create AI-native destinations that feel simple while preserving the identity and storytelling of each participant.

**Invisible Complexity. Visible Intelligence.** AI is a first-class path, not the only path. The opening hero may introduce ORB/voice/text interaction; the page remains a real, scrollable, indexable experience with conventional navigation.

## 2. Interaction model: Ask → Execute → Result
**Ask:** understand intent/context and ask only essential clarifying questions.
**Execute:** show a concise plan, obtain necessary permissions/approvals and perform authorized work; communicate meaningful progress.
**Result:** show what changed, evidence, what remains, costs and next action. Separate verified results from suggestions.

## 3. Landing-page composition
**Hero/Intelligence Gateway:** destination identity, proposition, primary action, optional ORB/voice/text input and a conventional way to explore. It must not make every brand look like a generic chatbot.

**Storytelling sections:** composable mission, offerings, people/expertise, activities, evidence, community, events, membership, contact, FAQ and policy patterns. No universal mandatory sequence.

**Dynamic AI presentation:** tailored text/cards/images/video/comparisons from approved destination knowledge and capabilities. State when information is unavailable or stale; never fabricate.

## 4. Archetypes
World/Ecosystem; Company; Community; Professional/Provider; Creator/Individual; Mission/Movement. Archetypes are composable starting points, not restrictive categories. A destination keeps its own brand and content.

## 5. ORB and destination personality
ORB is a shared interaction motif, not one universal visual personality. Destination tone may vary—e.g. Exafy professional/technical, Happy Melian warm/personal, Vitanaland exploratory, Healthy Million mission/evidence-led—while preserving universal Vitana AI identity and policy.

## 6. Visual/content standards
Prefer authentic, relevant real-life activity imagery. Avoid artificial stock imagery that undermines credibility. Respect rights, consent and provenance. Do not imply unverified health outcomes, endorsements or credentials. Content remains editable, versioned and accessible.

## 7. Navigation and discoverability
Support semantic headings, accessible routes, metadata, canonical URLs, indexable public content, structured data where appropriate, mobile behavior and performance. AI answers complement crawlable content. Private/member content is excluded from public indexing and retrieval.

## 8. Personalization and privacy
Use visitor context only under consent and policy. Keep public destination content, member-only content, personal context, operational data and sensitive records separate. Conventional browsing should not require unnecessary personal disclosure.

## 9. Builder preview and component system
Preview desktop/mobile, public/member views, AI behavior, navigation, commerce/booking journeys, accessibility and performance. Components define data contracts, responsive/accessibility behavior, themes, dependencies, permissions and empty/loading/error states. Use versioned approved components, not arbitrary executable code.

## 10. Acceptance criteria
Hero AI is configurable; storytelling/navigation remain; destination identity is preserved; answers use approved sources; public/private boundaries hold; mobile/accessibility/search are first-class; AI/integration outages degrade gracefully; preview matches published behavior; components are versioned.

## 11. Open decisions
Page/section schema; component catalogue; ORB modality/states; design tokens; dynamic rendering protocol; SEO/structured data; personalization consent; accessibility/performance targets; media rights.

**Guardrail:** do not redesign all existing apps at once. Validate patterns with reference destinations and preserve ongoing application work.
